# Vector Spaces and Bilinear Forms

## Key definitions

A ring is a set, $R$ with commutative addition and a not-necessarily commutative multiplication which interact with one another via the distributive law. A ring must obey the following axioms:
- There is a closed, binary addition operation
- Addition is associative
- Addition has identity
- Addition has inverses
- Addition is commutative
- There is a closed, binary multiplication operation
- Multiplication is associative
- Multiplication has identity
- The distributive law holds

Note that $(R,+)$ forms an Abelian group and $(R,\cdot)$ forms a semigroup.

#### Examples of Rings
- $\mathbb{Z}$ - the usual integers
- $\mathbb{Z} / n$ - $\mathbb{Z} \pmod{n}$
- $\mathbb{Z}[x]$ - $\mathbb{Z}$ polynomials in $x$
- $\mathbb{Z}[\alpha]$ - $\mathbb{Z}$ polynomials in $\alpha$ with $\alpha \in \overline{\mathbb{Q}}$
- $\mathbb{Z}[[x]]$ - formal power series over $\mathbb{Z}$

### Fields
A field, $F$, is a ring with extra structure:
1. Multiplication is commutative
2. Every non-zero element has a reciprocal.

#### Examples of Fields
- $\mathbb{Q}$ - the rationals
- $\mathbb{F}_{p}=\mathbb{Z}/p$ - The integers modulo $p$ where $p$ is a prime
- $\mathbb{Q}(x)$ - Rational functions in one variable
- $\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)$ - Rational functions in $\alpha$ where $\alpha \in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$. Also known as 'number fields'
- $\mathbb{Q}_{p}$ - the $p$-adic numbers
- $\mathbb{R}$ - the real numbers
- $\mathbb{C}$ the complex numbers

A vector space over some field, $F$ is a set, $V$ equipped with addition and scalar multiplication such that:
- $(V,+)$ forms a commutative group
- Scalar multiplication of vectors satisfies the standard laws
