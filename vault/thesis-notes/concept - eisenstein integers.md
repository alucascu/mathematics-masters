# Eisenstein Integers

## Statement / definition
An Eisenstein Integer, $\mathbb{Z}[\omega]$, is a complex number of the form
$$
p=a+b\omega
$$
Where $\omega=\exp\left( \frac{2\pi i}{3} \right)$.

An Eisenstein integer is prime if one of the following conditions is met:
1. $p=1-\omega$
2. If $b=0$, $a+b\omega$ is prime when $a$ is a prime with $a\equiv 2\pmod{3}$
3. Numbers of the form $a+b\omega$ or $a+b\omega^2$ where $(a+b\omega)(a+b\omega^2)=a^2-ab+b^2$ is a prime with $p=1\pmod{3}$

Since primes for which condition 3 is met are all of the form $p=u^2+3v^2$, finding the corresponding $u,v$ gives $a=u+v$ and $b=2v$

## Why it matters (for thesis)
The Eisenstein integers are the natural space to ask questions about cubic reciprocity.
