# Gaussian Integers
## Statement / definition
The Gauss integers, $\mathbb{Z}[i]$, are complex numbers of the form 
$$
	p=a+bi
$$

A Gaussian integer is prime if it adheres to one of the following criteria:
1. If $a,b \neq 0$, $a+bi$ is prime when $a^2+b^2$ is an ordinary prime in $\mathbb{Z}$
2. If $a=0$, $bi$ is prime if and only if $|b|$ is prime in $\mathbb{Z}$ and  $|b|\equiv 3 \pmod{4}$.
3. If $b=0$, $a$ is prime if and only if $|a|$ is an ordinary prime in $\mathbb{Z}$ and $|a|=3\pmod{4}$

## Why it matters (for thesis)
The natural domain for questions about quartic reciprocity laws is in the [[Ring]] of Gauss Integers.
